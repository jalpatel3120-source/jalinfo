A different way to see nature

Quantum mechanics describes physical systems at microscopic scales. Instead of always assigning a definite classical path, it uses states, probabilities and quantized physical quantities.

02 • GSEB Std. 12 Physics

Chapter 8 — Electromagnetic Waves

The current GSEB chapter listing places Electromagnetic Waves at Chapter 8. This section is designed as a visual revision guide.

⚡ What are EM waves?

Electromagnetic waves are oscillating electric and magnetic fields that propagate through space. In vacuum they travel at the speed of light.

c = 1 / √(μ₀ε₀)

🧲 Maxwell's idea

A changing electric field can be associated with a magnetic field, and changing magnetic fields are associated with electric fields. This leads to self-propagating electromagnetic waves.

📡 Spectrum

From lower to higher frequency: radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma ray.

03 • Atomic Physics

From Rutherford to Bohr

For completeness, the GSEB textbook sequence also includes Atoms as a later chapter. These concepts connect naturally to quantum ideas.

Thomson model

The early model pictured positive charge distributed through the atom with electrons embedded in it.

Rutherford scattering

Alpha-particle scattering led to the nuclear model: most of the atom is empty space, with positive charge concentrated in a small nucleus.

Bohr model

Bohr introduced allowed stationary states and quantized angular momentum for the hydrogen atom.

L = nh / 2π

Hydrogen energy levels

For hydrogen in the Bohr model, the bound-state energy is often written as:

Eₙ = −13.6 / n² eV
